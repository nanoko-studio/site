What is compound interest?

With compound interest, the interest you earn is added to your balance and starts earning interest itself. The longer the money stays invested, the faster it grows: in the example below more than half of the final value is interest.

FV = P × (1 + r/k)k×t + PMT × ((1 + r/k)k×t − 1) ÷ (r/k)
P: initial amount · PMT: contribution each period · r: annual rate · k: compounding periods a year · t: years

The formula is exact when you contribute once per compounding period, at the end of it. The calculator also handles contributions and compounding at different frequencies, by simulating every month.

Example: 10,000 plus 500 a month at 7% for 20 years

Start 10 years earlier and keep going for 30 years, and the same plan reaches 691,150.46 - you put in only 190,000.00 in total, but the balance more than doubles.

Does compounding frequency matter?

A little. With the same inputs, monthly compounding gives 300,850.70, quarterly 299,257.85 and yearly 292,561.44. Time and the rate matter far more than how often interest is added.

Fixed deposit: interest monthly or at maturity?

Monthly interest usually comes with a slightly lower rate, so if you don't need the income, interest at maturity rolled over with the principal tends to earn a little more. See the example and the break-even rate →

Savings example: 10,000 in a savings account for 5 years

A deposit of 10,000 at 4.5% a year, compounded monthly and left untouched, grows to 12,517.94 in 5 years, of which 2,517.94 is interest. If you withdrew the interest every month instead, you would earn only 2,250.00. The difference is what compounding adds.

Open this example in the calculator, then enter your own bank's rate. For a certificate of deposit that renews every year, set compounding to yearly. The rate here is only an illustration.

The rule of 72

Divide 72 by the annual rate to estimate how many years it takes to double your money. At 7% that is about 10 years; at 10%, about 7 years.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?
Simple interest is paid only on the money you put in. Compound interest is also paid on interest you have already earned, so the balance grows faster every year.
Does a savings account earn compound interest?
Yes, as long as you leave the interest in the account. Set the contribution to 0 and pick the compounding your bank uses: monthly for most savings accounts, or the term length for a certificate of deposit that renews with its interest.
How is the annual rate applied each month?
The annual rate is divided by the number of compounding periods: 7% compounded monthly is 7% ÷ 12 per month. This is the nominal rate most savings accounts and calculators use.
What does contribution timing change?
Contributions at the beginning of each period earn interest for that period too, so they end up slightly higher than contributions made at the end.
Can I use it for stocks or funds?
Yes, as an estimate: enter the average yearly return you expect. Real returns go up and down from year to year, so treat the result as a projection, not a promise.
Is what I type sent anywhere?
No. Every calculation runs in your browser. The numbers only appear in the page address so you can share your result.
