Flat rate vs reducing balance: which loan really costs more?

Nanoko Studio · Updated 20 Nov 2026

What is a flat interest rate?

With a flat rate, the interest is worked out on the full amount you originally borrowed, and it stays that way however much you have already paid back.

Monthly interest = Loan amount × Annual rate ÷ 12

Borrow 10,000 at a 12% flat rate and you pay 100.00 in interest every month - even in the final month, when you only owe about 837.00. Flat rates turn up mostly on personal loans, car finance and store instalment plans.

What is a reducing balance rate?

A reducing balance rate (also called a diminishing or effective rate) charges interest only on what you still owe. Each repayment shrinks the balance, so the interest shrinks with it. It usually comes in one of two forms:

  • Equal principal: you pay off the same slice of the loan every month, so the first payment is the largest and each one after is a little smaller.
  • Equal monthly payments: the payment never changes. Early on it is mostly interest; towards the end it is mostly principal. Most mortgages and bank car loans work this way.

In month 1 the flat-rate loan and the reducing-balance loan cost exactly the same. The gap opens up after that: on a flat rate the interest never drops, even though your balance does. Open this example in the loan calculator →

Why does a flat rate cost almost twice as much?

When you repay in monthly instalments over a year, you only have about half of the money for the year on average. A flat rate still charges interest on the full 10,000 the whole time.

So before comparing a flat-rate offer with a reducing-balance one, convert it to the effective rate: the reducing-balance rate that gives you exactly the same monthly payment. It is close to the APR lenders quote, although an APR may also include fees.

How to spot a flat rate in a loan offer

  • Flat rates are often labelled "flat rate p.a.", "add-on interest", or shown as a bare percentage next to a fixed monthly payment.
  • Reducing balance loans usually mention "interest on the outstanding balance", "reducing balance" or an APR.
  • The surest way is to ask for the repayment schedule and the total interest, then put them into the calculator to see the real rate.

Frequently asked questions

Is a flat rate ever the better deal?
Yes, if the flat rate is less than about half of the reducing-balance rate you have been offered. A 6% flat rate over 12 months works out to an effective rate of about 10.8% a year, which still beats a 12% reducing-balance loan. Compare effective rates or total interest, not the headline number.
Is a flat rate the same as a fixed rate?
No. A fixed rate means the rate does not change during the loan, and it is normally charged on a reducing balance. A flat rate is about what the interest is charged on: always the original amount.
Is 0% finance a flat rate?
With no interest, the method makes no difference. The real cost tends to hide in arrangement fees, insurance, or a price that is higher than paying upfront. Add those up before you compare.
What happens if I pay off a flat-rate loan early?
It depends on the contract. Some lenders only charge interest up to the settlement date plus a fee; others still charge part or all of the remaining interest. Check before you sign.
