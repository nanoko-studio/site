When the intro rate ends: how much more will you pay each month?

Nanoko Studio · Updated 21 Nov 2026

Borrow 300,000 over 25 years with equal monthly payments, 4.5% fixed for 24 months, then a variable rate of, say, 6.5%:

Month 241,667last intro month, at 4.5%
Month 252,002first variable month, at 6.5%
After the intro rate, you pay+335more a month, up 20.1% overnight

Most mortgages open with an attractive number: a fixed or discounted rate for the first two to five years. What fewer people work out in advance is the first month after that deal ends, when the loan moves to a variable rate and the monthly payment resets to a new level.

How the variable rate is set

After the intro period, the rate usually follows a reference rate plus a margin. The reference may be the central bank rate, a market rate or the lender's own standard variable rate, and the margin is fixed in your contract. When the reference moves, your rate and your payment move with it.

Nobody knows what the reference will be in two or five years. The safe way to plan is to try a few variable rates, as below, rather than look only at the intro rate.

Example: 300,000 at 4.5% for 2 years

The loan runs 25 years with equal monthly payments. For the first 24 months you pay 1,667 a month. In month 25 the rate goes to 6.5% and the payment is recalculated on the remaining balance and term: it becomes 2,002, 335 more every month, a rise of 20.1%.

How big that jump is depends on the variable rate. The table keeps the 4.5% intro rate for 24 months and changes only the variable rate:

Each extra 1% on the variable rate adds about 178 to the month 25 payment and about 49,220 to the total interest over 25 years. If the whole loan were at 6.5% with no intro deal, total interest would be 307,499, so the two intro years save only about 14,792. Most of the interest is paid in the variable years.

The lowest intro rate is not always the cheapest deal

Lenders often offer a choice between a lower rate for a short time and a slightly higher rate for longer. Three deals on the same 300,000 over 25 years:

Intro dealTotal interest over 25 years if the variable rate is

The 3.9% deal looks best, but it lasts only 12 months, so with a 6.5% variable rate it costs about 18,619 more than 4.9% fixed for 5 years. When the variable rate is well above the intro rate, how long the deal lasts usually matters more than the rate itself. Compare total interest at a few variable rates before choosing, and check the early repayment charges of each deal: longer deals tend to lock you in for longer.

What to do before the intro rate ends

  • Work out the payment at a variable rate 2-3% higher than you expect, and only borrow what you could still pay in that case.
  • Set money aside to overpay when the deal ends. Early repayment charges usually stop with the intro period, and every pound or dollar you pay off then saves interest at the full variable rate. Is paying off a loan early worth it? →
  • Ask exactly how the variable rate is set: which reference rate, what margin, how often it changes, and where that is written in the contract.
  • A few months before the deal ends, compare remortgage offers, counting arrangement, valuation and legal fees as well as the rate.

Frequently asked questions

How is a variable mortgage rate calculated?
Usually as a reference rate plus a fixed margin. The reference may be the central bank rate, a market rate or the lender's standard variable rate. The exact formula and how often it changes are in your loan contract.
How much will my payment go up when the intro rate ends?
It depends on the gap between the intro and variable rates. On 300,000 over 25 years, 4.5% for two years then 6.5%, the payment rises by about 335 a month, around a fifth. Use the calculator above for your own loan.
Should I choose a short low-rate deal or a longer higher-rate one?
When the variable rate is well above the intro rate, the longer deal is usually cheaper over the whole loan. Compare total interest at a few variable rates and check how long the early repayment charges last.
Should I pay down the loan when the intro period ends?
If you have spare cash and the early repayment charges have ended, paying down then lowers both the monthly payment and the total interest. Keep an emergency fund of a few months' spending first.
